[ 01 ] // ABOUT

Who I am

I'm a computational biologist who turns biological images into decisions — which genetic variants matter, which drugs could rescue a broken cell, and which chemicals are safe. I currently co-direct the Carpenter–Shen Lab at Purdue University with Anne Carpenter, building open methods and software for image-based discovery.

My path runs from evolution to medicine: population genetics at Cornell, where I derived the theory behind bulk segregant analysis; industry-scale bioinformatics at BeiGene across more than 30 drug programs; and the imaging platform at the Broad Institute, where I worked on variant characterization (VarChAMP), chemical safety without animal testing (OASIS), rare-disease drug repurposing (VISTA), and cell-representation benchmarks (JUMP).

I care about open, collaborative science — the kind where methods, data, and tools are shared so discoveries can compound.

[ 03 ] // RESEARCH

What I work on

VARCHAMPconsortium

Which variants matter?

Machine-learning pipelines detect protein mislocalization and shape changes across 1,600+ coding variants from Cell Painting profiles — and translate them into pathogenicity estimates for clinical interpretation.

OASISconsortium

Chemicals without animal testing

Predicting human and rat hepatotoxicity from high-dimensional cell profiles, laying the groundwork for replacing animal testing in medicine and agriculture.

VISTAconsortium

New cures for rare disease

Optical pooled screening to find FDA-approved drugs that rescue disease-causing protein mislocalization — turning existing medicines into new treatments.

JUMPcommunity

Benchmarks for cell representations

Co-developing community benchmarks and a compact reference dataset from JUMP, the world's largest public Cell Painting dataset, for rapid model training and evaluation.

FOUNDATIONSgenetics

Population genetics

Analytical solutions for the genomic resolution of bulk segregant analysis (G3, 2022) and selection patterns in Drosophila–Wolbachia coevolution (Evolution, 2023).
